Northwest Arkansas at a glance

When people say “NWA,” they usually mean the string of cities along I-49 in the northwest corner of Arkansas: Fayetteville, Springdale, Rogers and Bentonville, plus smaller towns like Lowell, Centerton, Bella Vista and Cave Springs. It’s one connected region. Most people live in one town, work in another and drive across the region for dinner without thinking twice.

Northwest Arkansas is a two-county region (Benton and Washington) whose four largest cities, Fayetteville, Springdale, Rogers and Bentonville, had about 337,000 residents combined in July 2025 (U.S. Census Bureau estimates).

Northwest Arkansas National Airport (XNA) is the region’s main airport, the Razorback Greenway trail connects the major cities, and the Ozark Mountains, Beaver Lake and the Buffalo National River are all within easy reach for weekends.

Moving to Northwest Arkansas: what you need to know

Why people move here

Major employers

Walmart’s headquarters in Bentonville, Tyson Foods in Springdale and J.B. Hunt in Lowell, plus the hundreds of supplier offices that support them.

Education & health care

The University of Arkansas in Fayetteville and major health systems with hospitals and clinics across the region.

Outdoors & culture

Crystal Bridges, the Walton Arts Center, Razorback athletics, hundreds of miles of trails, and lakes and rivers nearby.

What I hear most from people who move here is that they didn’t expect so much to do in a region this size: trails, restaurants, Crystal Bridges, Razorback games and lakes within an hour. The surprise after they arrive is usually how spread out it is. You’ll drive between towns more than you expect, so where you live compared to where you work matters a lot.

Which NWA town fits you?

Each town has its own personality. Here’s the short version. Click any town for listings, neighborhoods and schools:

  • Bentonville: trails, Crystal Bridges, downtown square and Walmart’s Home Office. Higher prices, high demand.
  • Rogers: shopping, dining, the Walmart AMP and a historic downtown. A wide range of price points.
  • Fayetteville: the University of Arkansas, Dickson Street and a college-town feel.
  • Springdale: central location, Tyson Foods and a revitalized downtown. Often more home for the money.
  • Lowell: a small town between Rogers and Springdale, home to J.B. Hunt.
  • Centerton: newer neighborhoods and new construction just west of Bentonville.
  • Bella Vista: lakes, golf courses, wooded lots and mountain bike trails north of Bentonville.
  • Cave Springs: larger lots and newer homes in the middle of the region.

My quick rule of thumb: if you want walkability and things to do, look at downtown Bentonville or Fayetteville. If you want more house for the money, look at Springdale, Lowell or parts of Rogers. If you want newer construction and a yard, look at Centerton or Cave Springs. If you want lakes, golf and trees, look at Bella Vista.

What it costs to live here

  • Home prices: each town’s page shows its county’s median sale price for the month from the NWA Home Report, based on Northwest Arkansas Board of REALTORS® MLS data. Bentonville and Cave Springs usually run above the regional median, and Springdale, Lowell and Bella Vista often come in below it. Based on information from the Northwest Arkansas Board of REALTORS® for the period September 1, 2026 through September 30, 2026.
  • Property taxes: Arkansas offers a homestead property tax credit on your primary residence. Ask your county assessor or me about current amounts. In Arkansas, your tax is based on 20% of your home’s assessed value times the local millage rate, which varies by city and school district. The homestead credit is now $675 a year, and the legislature has raised it several years in a row. Your lender will estimate taxes in your Loan Estimate, and I can pull the current tax bill on any home you’re considering.
  • Utilities: Electric, water and internet providers vary by town and even by street. I can tell you who serves a specific address. Electric service in NWA comes mostly from SWEPCO, Carroll Electric Cooperative and Ozarks Electric Cooperative, depending on where you live. For internet, Cox and AT&T Fiber cover most of the cities, and OzarksGo offers fiber across much of the Ozarks Electric area. Water and trash usually come from your city.
  • Personal property: Arkansas requires you to assess vehicles and other personal property with your county assessor every year. It’s an easy step new residents often miss.

Buying a home from out of state

Many of my clients buy before they ever move here. Here’s how it works:

  • Start with a call. We talk through your job location, commute, schools, budget and lifestyle, and I narrow it to the right towns and neighborhoods.
  • Get pre-approved early. I can connect you with local lenders who know NWA.
  • Tour by video. I’ll walk homes for you on live video and point out what photos don’t show.
  • Plan one trip. If you can visit, we’ll make it count with a tour of your top homes and neighborhoods.
  • Sign and close remotely. Most paperwork can be signed electronically, and the title company can usually arrange a remote closing.

Moving to Northwest Arkansas: FAQ

Is Northwest Arkansas a good place to live?

For most people, yes. You get strong jobs from Walmart, Tyson, J.B. Hunt and the University of Arkansas, hundreds of miles of trails, good schools and a lower cost of living than most of the fast-growing metros people move from. The trade-offs come from that same growth: more traffic, more construction and home prices that have climbed a lot over the past decade.

What’s the best town in NWA to live in?

It depends on where you work and how you want to live. A few quick examples: if you work at the Walmart Home Office and want to bike to work, look at Bentonville. If you’re at the University or want a college-town feel, look at Fayetteville. If you want a newer home with a yard and an easy commute, look at Centerton or Cave Springs. If you want the most house for your budget in the middle of the region, look at Springdale or Lowell.

How expensive is it to live in Northwest Arkansas?

Home prices here are lower than in most of the big metros people relocate from, but they’ve climbed quickly. In September 2026 the median sale price was $363,600 in Benton County and $350,000 in Washington County. Check the market snapshot on any town page for this month’s median sale price, and ask me what your budget buys in the towns you’re considering.

Source: Benton and Washington County residential closings for September 2026, from Northwest Arkansas Board of REALTORS® MLS data reported by NWA Look. Based on information from the Northwest Arkansas Board of REALTORS® for the period September 1, 2026 through September 30, 2026.

Can I buy a home in NWA before I move?

Yes. Many of my clients do. We tour by live video, sign electronically, and the title company can usually arrange a remote closing. I’ve helped buyers relocate from across the country, including a couple who bought their home here from Baton Rouge before they ever moved.

What’s the weather like in Northwest Arkansas?

NWA has four real seasons: warm, humid summers, colorful falls, generally mild winters with occasional snow and ice, and stormy springs. Spring is the season to watch. We get strong storms and the occasional tornado warning, so when you buy, check the roof, the drainage and where your safe spot in the house is. Fall is the best time of year here.
