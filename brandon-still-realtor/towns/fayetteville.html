What it’s like to live in Fayetteville

Fayetteville is the home of the Razorbacks, and the energy shows. Dickson Street has restaurants, music and the Walton Arts Center, the downtown square hosts the farmers market, and game days bring the whole town out.

Lake Fayetteville, Mount Sequoyah, Kessler Mountain and the Razorback Greenway keep the outdoors close, and each part of town has its own character, from historic streets near downtown to newer subdivisions on the west side.

Culture
Dickson Street, the Walton Arts Center and the farmers market on the square.
Outdoors

Fayetteville neighborhoods

Where you buy in Fayetteville changes the price, the lot size and the commute. A quick guide:

  • Downtown and Dickson Street

    Walkable, with older homes and newer infill. Usually some of the highest prices per square foot in town.

  • Near campus

    Steady rental demand from students and staff, with more turnover. Investors here should be ready to manage it.

  • Established neighborhoods

    Mid-century and 1990s homes on bigger lots, often a better value than downtown.

  • West Fayetteville and new construction

    Newer subdivisions with quick I-49 access.

Fayetteville schools

Most of Fayetteville is served by Fayetteville Public Schools.

School boundaries change, and some addresses fall in a different district than you’d expect. Confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the district before you buy; Brandon can help you check.

Official district information: the Arkansas Division of Elementary and Secondary Education, on dese.ade.arkansas.gov (opens in a new tab)

Buying an investment property in Fayetteville

Fayetteville has steady rental demand, including student rentals. Those come with more turnover and a school-year calendar: most student leases start in August, so plan the purchase and the leasing around it.

Check zoning and occupancy limits before planning to rent by the room. For nightly stays, the city requires a short-term rental license and splits them into Type 1, a primary residence, and Type 2, a whole home with no one living there. Brandon’s investing notes put the Type 2 cap at 475, with a waitlist, so confirm the current count with the city before you buy.

Fayetteville real estate FAQ

How much does a home cost in Fayetteville, AR?

Washington County’s median sale price was $350,000 in September 2026 (snapshot above). Brandon’s 48 Fayetteville sales on his Zillow record run from the under $200K range to the $1M and up range, and the most common were under $200K and $500K to $749K, 11 each. For a specific street, ask Brandon what sold within a mile. That is the number that matters.

Is Fayetteville a good place to live?

It depends on what you want close by. You get Dickson Street, the square, the trails and a lot going on. The trade-offs Brandon points to are game-day traffic, busy areas near campus, and prices near downtown that have climbed a lot in the last few years.

Is Fayetteville a good place to buy a rental property?

There is steady demand, including from students and staff. Expect more turnover near campus, plan leasing around an August start, and check zoning and occupancy limits before renting by the room.

Does buying a home for a college student make sense?

It can beat four years of rent, especially if the student rents the extra bedrooms to roommates. Look for something close to campus or on a bus route, with parking and enough bedrooms to share, and run the resale numbers too.

Can I run a short-term rental in Fayetteville?

Only with a city short-term rental license. Type 1 covers a primary residence and Type 2 a whole home with no one living there, and Type 2 licenses are capped. Check the current rules and waitlist with the city before you buy, not after.
