What it’s like to live in Bentonville

Bentonville is home to Walmart’s headquarters, Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art and the Momentary, and a trail system that has made it one of the best-known mountain biking towns in the country.

The downtown square is the center of it all, with restaurants, coffee shops, the farmers market and events most weekends. The Razorback Greenway runs through town and connects Bentonville to Rogers, Springdale and Fayetteville.

Trails and outdoors
Paved and mountain bike trails, including Slaughter Pen and Coler Mountain Bike Preserve, plus the Razorback Greenway.
Arts and culture
Crystal Bridges and the Momentary, plus concerts, festivals and events on the square through the year.
Work and commute
The Walmart Home Office campus, supplier offices and a growing health care sector. From most of Bentonville it is about 15 minutes to Rogers, 20 to XNA and 35 to 40 to Fayetteville, depending on traffic.

Drive times are Brandon’s estimates and change with traffic.

Bentonville neighborhoods

Where you buy in Bentonville changes the price, the lot size and the commute. A quick guide:

  • Downtown and the square

    Walkable to restaurants, Crystal Bridges and the trails. Older homes, new infill and townhomes, usually at the highest price per square foot in town.

  • Southwest Bentonville

    Newer subdivisions toward Centerton, often a newer home for less than closer to downtown.

  • North Bentonville toward Bella Vista

    More established neighborhoods, bigger lots and mature trees, with quick access to the Slaughter Pen and Bella Vista trail systems.

  • New construction

    Mostly on the southwest side and along the Centerton line, from national and local builders. HOAs are common, so read the covenants, especially if the home could be a rental later.

Bentonville schools

Most of Bentonville is served by Bentonville Public Schools.

School boundaries change, and some addresses fall in a different district than you’d expect. Confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the district before you buy; Brandon can help you check.

Official district information: the Arkansas Division of Elementary and Secondary Education, on dese.ade.arkansas.gov (opens in a new tab)

Buying an investment property in Bentonville

Brandon’s own start was here: his first property sits near Walmart’s new Home Office campus, and he picked it for location over the number of doors. He tells that story on NWA Investing, Ep. 61, below.

The catch in Bentonville is purchase price. Cash flow is tighter than in Springdale or Rogers, so many Bentonville rentals are more of an appreciation play. Check HOA rules before you buy, since some newer neighborhoods restrict leasing.

Bentonville real estate FAQ

How much does a home cost in Bentonville, AR?

Benton County’s median sale price was $363,600 in September 2026 (snapshot above). Brandon’s 33 Bentonville sales on his Zillow record run from the $200K to $299K range to the $1M and up range, and the most common was $300K to $399K (18 of 33). Downtown and newer homes near the trails sit at the top end, and older homes and townhomes are where the lower prices are. Centerton and parts of Rogers often get more house for the money. For a specific street, ask Brandon what sold within a mile. That is the number that matters.

Is Bentonville a good place to live?

There is a lot to do for a town this size, with the trails, the square and Crystal Bridges close together. The trade-offs, in Brandon’s words: “prices are higher than most of NWA, and growth means traffic and construction that weren’t here ten years ago.”

Should I buy in Bentonville or Rogers?

They share a border, and the drive between them is about 15 minutes. Some west Rogers addresses are in Bentonville’s school district, so check the specific address. Brandon’s short version: “If being in the middle of Bentonville’s energy is the reason you’re moving here, buy in Bentonville and don’t look back. If you want space, value and an easy drive to everything, Rogers is hard to beat.”

Who is building new homes in Bentonville?

The builders Brandon sees most are Lennar (which bought Rausch Coleman Homes), D.R. Horton, Schuber Mitchell Homes and local builders like Baumann & Crosno. Toll Brothers came into the area in 2026 by buying Buffington Homes, so many luxury builds now carry the Buffington Homes by Toll Brothers name. Ask about builder incentives before you sign.
