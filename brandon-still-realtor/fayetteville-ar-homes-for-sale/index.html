Living in Fayetteville, AR

What it’s like to live in Fayetteville

Fayetteville is home to the University of Arkansas, and the energy shows. Dickson Street has restaurants, music and the Walton Arts Center, the downtown square hosts one of the region’s best farmers markets, and game days bring the whole town out. Lake Fayetteville, Mount Sequoyah and the Razorback Greenway keep the outdoors close.

Fayetteville fits people who want energy: game days, Dickson Street, the farmers market and trails right out the back door. What surprises people is how different each part of town feels, from the historic streets near downtown to newer subdivisions on the west side. Right now I like the value in established neighborhoods a little farther from campus, where you skip the student-rental premium.

University & culture

The University of Arkansas, Razorback athletics, the Walton Arts Center and Dickson Street.

Outdoors

Lake Fayetteville, Mount Sequoyah, Kessler Mountain and miles of paved and dirt trails.

Character

Older neighborhoods near downtown, newer subdivisions on the edges of town, and everything in between.

Fayetteville neighborhoods

Where you buy in Fayetteville changes your price, lot size, commute and school. Here’s a quick guide:

  • Downtown & Dickson Street: walkable, older homes and newer infill. Usually some of the highest prices per square foot in town.
  • Near the university: strong rental demand from students and staff, and more turnover. Great for investors who are ready to manage it.
  • Established neighborhoods across town: mid-century and 1990s homes on bigger lots, often a better value than downtown.
  • West Fayetteville & new construction: newer subdivisions with quick I-49 access. HOAs are common, so read the covenants before you buy, especially if you might rent it out later.

Fayetteville schools

Most of Fayetteville is served by Fayetteville Public Schools, including Fayetteville High School. School boundaries can change and some addresses fall in a different district than you’d expect, so always confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the district before you buy. I’m happy to help you check.

Fayetteville real estate FAQ

How much does a home cost in Fayetteville, AR?

Zillow’s typical home value in Fayetteville was $379,367 on Sept. 30, 2026, up 3.6% from a year earlier. Across Washington County, the median sale price was $350,000 in September 2026. Fayetteville has a wide range, from older homes and condos near campus to higher-end homes near downtown and on the hills. Homes closest to downtown and the university usually cost the most per square foot. For your specific street, ask me what sold within a mile. That’s the number that actually matters.

Is Fayetteville a good place to live?

Yes, especially if you want a college-town feel with real culture: the University of Arkansas, the Walton Arts Center, a great farmers market and trails everywhere. The trade-offs are game-day traffic, student-heavy areas near campus, and prices near downtown that have climbed a lot in the last few years.

Is Fayetteville a good place to buy a rental property?

It can be. Demand from the university and nearby employers is steady. Student rentals can cash flow well, but expect more turnover, more wear and a leasing calendar that follows the school year. Long-term rentals in established neighborhoods are steadier. Check zoning and occupancy limits before you plan to rent by the room.

Is it a good idea to buy a home for a student at the University of Arkansas?

For a lot of buyers it beats four years of rent, especially if the student rents out the extra bedrooms to roommates. Look for something close to campus or on a bus route, with parking and enough bedrooms to share. Run the numbers on resale too, since you’ll likely sell or rent it out after graduation. I’m happy to walk you through it.

Where is new construction in Fayetteville?

Most new construction is on the west side and the edges of town, with some infill closer to downtown. Riverwood Homes, which got its start in Fayetteville, is one of the most active builders here, including a 55+ community. Baumann & Crosno also has new communities on the west side. With more inventory on the market this fall, some builders are offering incentives, so ask before you sign.
