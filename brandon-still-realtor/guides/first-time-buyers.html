First-Time Home Buyer Guide for Northwest Arkansas

Buying your first home is a big step. This guide walks through every part of it in plain English, from your budget to the day you get your keys, with the NWA-specific details most guides leave out.

The 8 steps to buying your first home.

  1. Set your budget

    Look at your monthly payment, not just the price: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA dues.

    Mortgage payment calculator

  2. Get pre-approved

    A lender looks at your income, credit and savings and tells you what you can borrow. Sellers expect it with your offer.

  3. Pick your agent

    You’ll sign a written buyer agreement before touring. It spells out what your agent does for you and how they’re paid.

  4. Shop for homes

    Tour the homes that fit. Brandon points out what to watch for: roofs, foundations, drainage and resale.

  5. Make an offer

    Write a strong offer based on recent sales, with earnest money and the right contingencies. A seller credit toward your closing costs or a rate buydown can be part of it too.

  6. Inspect and appraise

    A home inspection checks the condition, and the lender’s appraisal confirms the value.

  7. Final loan approval

    Your lender finalizes the loan. Don’t open new credit or make big purchases during this time.

  8. Close and get your keys

    Sign at the title company, the funds transfer, and the home is yours.

Loan options for first-time buyers.

The right loan depends on your credit, savings and where you’re buying. Common options:

Conventional

Some programs for first-time buyers allow a low down payment.

FHA

A government-backed loan with a smaller down payment than most conventional loans and more flexible credit guidelines. FHA loans on hud.gov (opens in a new tab)

VA

For eligible veterans and service members, with a low or no down payment option. VA home loans on va.gov (opens in a new tab)

USDA

For eligible buyers in qualifying rural areas, with a low or no down payment option. Some areas around NWA may qualify. USDA home loans on rd.usda.gov (opens in a new tab)

Down payment assistance

The Arkansas Development Finance Authority (ADFA) offers programs that can help with your down payment and closing costs. Ask your lender which ones you qualify for.

Loan programs, limits and terms change and vary by lender, and your lender confirms what you qualify for. General information, not financial advice.

What it costs to buy.

Down payment

Depends on the loan program. Your lender will tell you what yours requires.

Earnest money

A good-faith deposit when your offer is accepted, credited back to you at closing.

Inspection

Paid up front, usually a few hundred dollars. Worth every penny.

Appraisal

Ordered by your lender.

Closing costs

Lender and title fees, prepaid taxes and insurance. Your lender gives you a Loan Estimate with the details. Sometimes you can negotiate for the seller to cover part of them.

Pro tip: ask for a seller credit toward a buydown

“If you ask for $10,000 off of the purchase price of a $475,000 home, you’ll save $70 a month on your payment. If you ask the seller for a $10,000 credit to buy down your interest rate, you’ll save $319 a month!”

How a seller credit works

First-time buyer mistakes Brandon sees most.

  • Shopping before getting pre-approved.
  • Opening a new credit card or financing furniture before closing.
  • Skipping the inspection to win a bidding war.
  • Forgetting to budget for taxes, insurance and HOA dues.
  • Changing jobs or moving large amounts of money between accounts while your loan is in process.

First-time home buyer questions.

How much do I need for a down payment?

It depends on the loan. Some programs allow a low down payment, and Arkansas down payment assistance through ADFA can help with it and with closing costs. Your lender can tell you exactly what each program asks for, and Brandon can walk you through how each one affects your offer.

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

It depends on the loan type and lender. A lender can tell you where you stand and what to improve. If your score needs work, a good lender will tell you exactly what to fix and how long it should take.

How long does it take to buy a home?

Plan on about 30 to 45 days from an accepted offer to closing for most loans. The search before that depends on you and the market. Some buyers find the right home in a weekend, and others take a few months. Getting pre-approved first is what keeps the whole thing moving.

Do I have to pay my real estate agent?

You’ll sign a written buyer agreement before touring homes. It spells out how your agent is paid. That amount is negotiable, and in many cases the seller agrees to cover some or all of it. Brandon walks you through it before you start.

Should I rent or buy in Northwest Arkansas?

Compare your rent to a full mortgage payment, including taxes, insurance and any HOA dues, not just principal and interest. If the payment is close and you plan to stay at least three to five years, buying usually comes out ahead because you’re building equity instead of paying your landlord’s mortgage. If you might move in a year or two, renting is often the smarter call. Brandon can run the numbers for your situation.

General information, not legal, tax or financial advice.
