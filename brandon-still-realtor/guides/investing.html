Brandon owns rentals and multifamily across Northwest Arkansas and co-hosts the NWA Investing podcast. This guide covers what he would want to know before buying a first, or a next, investment property here.

Why investors look at Northwest Arkansas.

Northwest Arkansas isn’t a one-company town, even though Walmart gets most of the attention. Three Fortune 500 headquarters sit within a few miles of each other: Walmart in Bentonville, Tyson Foods in Springdale and J.B. Hunt in Lowell. Add the university in Fayetteville, hundreds of supplier offices and a growing health care sector, and you get steady demand for housing across price points.

Six ways to invest in NWA.

Single-family rentals

The easiest place to start. Tenants tend to stay longer, and resale is the easiest, since your buyer pool includes homeowners. The numbers usually work out better in Springdale, Lowell, Rogers and Bella Vista, where prices are lower compared to rents. Bentonville and newer homes in Centerton tend to be more of an appreciation play.

Duplexes to fourplexes

Several rents under one roof. If you live in one unit, owner-occupied financing such as FHA can apply to properties up to four units, which is one of the best ways to get started.

“Live in one side. Let the other unit pay down your mortgage every month.” Brandon, on Instagram, Sep 2026

Multifamily, 5 units and up

Valued on income, not comparable sales, so better management means more value. Usually financed with commercial or portfolio loans. The biggest lever is operations: tighten up management, bring rents to market and control expenses, and the value follows.

“Especially in Northwest Arkansas there’s a lot of meat on the bone to be able to ... add value.” Brandon, NWA Investing Ep. 60, Jun 2025

Student rentals in Fayetteville

Strong demand near campus, with more turnover and wear. Buy close to campus or on a bus route, budget for the turnover, and plan your leasing around the school year, since most student leases start in August.

Short-term rentals

Can earn more per night, but every NWA city sets its own rules, permits and taxes, and HOAs can ban them. Check the city and the HOA before you buy, not after.

Ep. 97 on the short-term rental market

Value-add and BRRRR

Buy something that needs work, renovate, rent it, refinance and repeat. It works best when your rehab budget and after-repair value are realistic.

The numbers that matter.

Every deal comes down to a few numbers. Run all of them before you make an offer:

Net operating income (NOI)

Rent minus operating expenses (taxes, insurance, repairs, management, vacancy). It doesn’t include your mortgage.

Cap rate

NOI divided by purchase price. It’s useful for comparing properties, especially multifamily.

Cash flow

NOI minus your mortgage payment. This is what actually lands in your account each month.

Cash-on-cash return

Yearly cash flow divided by the cash you put in (down payment, closing costs and repairs).

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

NOI divided by yearly mortgage payments. Many investment lenders want this above about 1.2.

Budget for the expenses people forget: vacancy, repairs, capital items like roofs and HVAC, property management (even if you self-manage today), and insurance, which has been rising. As a starting point, budget about 5% to 8% of rent for vacancy, 5% to 10% for repairs, another 5% to 10% for capital items and 8% to 10% for management, then adjust for the age and condition of the property. Get real insurance quotes before you make an offer.

Financing an investment property.

Conventional investment loans

Typically a larger down payment than a home you live in.

Owner-occupied 2 to 4 units

Live in one unit and rent the others, which can open up lower down payment options like FHA.

DSCR loans

Qualify based on the property’s rent instead of your personal income.

Local bank and portfolio loans

NWA banks often lend on rentals and multifamily with more flexible terms.

Arkansas landlord basics.

Arkansas is generally considered landlord-friendly, but there are still rules. Brandon’s guide cites the Arkansas Attorney General for these:

  • Security deposits can’t be more than two months’ rent, and must be returned with an itemized list of any deductions within 60 days of move-out. This law applies to landlords with six or more units, and to any landlord who uses a paid property manager.
  • An eviction for unpaid rent (unlawful detainer) starts with a three-day written notice to vacate.
  • City building and housing codes apply, so check the rules in the city where you’re buying.

Laws change. Talk to an Arkansas real estate attorney before you write your lease or file an eviction. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

Investing in NWA: questions.

Is Northwest Arkansas a good place to invest in real estate?

Brandon thinks so. NWA has three Fortune 500 headquarters, a major university, steady population growth and strong rent demand. The trade-offs are real: prices have risen, cash flow is tighter than it was a few years ago, and insurance and taxes keep climbing. His advice is to buy for the long term and run conservative numbers.

How much do I need to buy my first rental in NWA?

It depends on the price and the loan. A conventional investment loan usually asks for a larger down payment than a home you live in, plus closing costs and a few months of payments in reserve. If you live in one side of a duplex, an owner-occupied loan such as FHA can lower what you need up front. Your lender can tell you the exact terms. Whatever you buy, keep cash on hand for the first big repair.

Should I self-manage or hire a property manager?

Self-managing saves the fee and teaches you the business, and it works fine for one or two properties close to home. Once you have more doors, a demanding job or tenants you’d rather not hear from at midnight, a good property manager usually pays for themselves. Either way, budget for management in your numbers so the deal still works if you hand it off later.

Are short-term rentals allowed in Bentonville and Fayetteville?

Each city sets its own rules, and many HOAs restrict short-term rentals. Check the city’s current ordinance and the HOA covenants before you buy. Fayetteville requires a short-term rental license and splits rentals into Type 1 (your primary residence) and Type 2 (a whole home with no one living there). Brandon’s 2026 guide notes that Type 2 licenses are capped at 475 and have a waitlist. Bentonville’s rules have historically been lighter, but confirm current permit and lodging tax requirements with the city before you buy.
