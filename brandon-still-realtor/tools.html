Ask for a seller credit.

Sellers sometimes agree to cover part of a buyer’s closing costs. Brandon’s tip is to point that money at the interest rate.

What it costs to buy

“If you ask for $10,000 off of the purchase price of a $475,000 home, you’ll save $70 a month on your payment. If you ask the seller for a $10,000 credit to buy down your interest rate, you’ll save $319 a month!”

Brandon’s tip, Selling with Still, Dec 2023

From his newsletter on beehiiv.com (opens in a new tab). The sample numbers here assume each point lowers the rate 0.25%, so the gap is smaller. Try your own numbers.

  • A seller credit is money the seller agrees to put toward your costs at closing. You ask for it in the offer, the same way you negotiate the price.
  • A rate buydown spends that money on points, which lower the interest rate on the loan. Some buydowns last the life of the loan and some are temporary; your lender tells you which one you are getting.
  • Each loan program caps how much a seller can contribute, and your lender decides how a credit can be used. Ask before you write the offer.

What each number means.

Definitions from Brandon’s investing guide.

Net operating income (NOI)

Rent minus operating expenses (taxes, insurance, repairs, management, vacancy). It doesn’t include your mortgage.

Cap rate

NOI divided by purchase price. It’s useful for comparing properties, especially multifamily.

Cash flow

NOI minus your mortgage payment. This is what actually lands in your account each month.

Cash-on-cash return

Yearly cash flow divided by the cash you put in (down payment, closing costs and repairs).

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

NOI divided by yearly mortgage payments. Many investment lenders want this above about 1.2.
